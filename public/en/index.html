The problem

You do not have a shortage of leads. You have an enquiry problem.

The enquiry comes in. What happens in the ten minutes after that decides who sells that car.

01

The enquiry comes in at 21:43

Someone sees your advert on the sofa in the evening and asks whether the car is still there. Your salesperson reads it tomorrow morning. By then they have asked three others too.

Whoever answers first, sells.

02

Your salesperson is already with a customer

A serious enquiry comes in while they are out on a test drive. It waits until things are quiet, and today they will not be.

A buyer who was waiting does not wait long.

03

The same five questions, all day long

Is it still there, how many kilometres, does it have a panoramic roof, can I come by on Saturday, what is the price. Your best salesperson retypes that thirty times a week.

Selling time that goes into typing.

04

The enquiry disappears into a mailbox

It is there all right, but nobody owns it. There is no file, no status and no next step. It simply sinks under the rest.

It exists, but it is not alive.

05

Following up hangs on someone's memory

"I'll think about it." Three days later nobody remembers who that was, which car it was about, or what was agreed.

The second chance never comes.

None of these five is about too little interest. They are all about buyers you already had.

From enquiry to appointment

Five steps. One system.

  1. 01

    A buyer asks about a car

    Through your website, WhatsApp or email. Inside or outside your opening hours makes no difference to the answer.

  2. 02

    Helvaro answers with the car attached

    It knows which vehicle they mean and pulls the data from your stock. Price, mileage, year and trim come from you, not from a guess.

  3. 03

    The conversation becomes a file

    Who it is, which car, what budget, what term, whether a trade-in is involved. What the buyer says goes into the file. What they do not say is not invented.

  4. 04

    Silence gets a follow-up

    "I'll think about it" is not an end point. A message follows, at a moment you decide, with the car and the conversation attached.

  5. 05

    Your salesperson takes over when it counts

    For a negotiation, a trade-in or a borderline case, a person steps into the running conversation with everything already said within reach.
